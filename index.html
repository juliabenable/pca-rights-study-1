The short version

Simple Skincare is our first paid campaign, and the payment is for rights. The contract defines those rights precisely. On the Sep 8 call Tony confirmed the contract's version and narrowed the fee.

Decided · Sep 8, 6:30pmTony, joining the end of the Julia and Katie call
  • The license is the contract's. "They're looking for full 90-day paid usage rights for all videos." Ads included, and ads stop at 90 days.
  • Tell creators plainly. "It should be full usage rights. Includes paid ads, organic, social, whatever, something like that. 90 days."
  • The payment buys the rights. Asked whether the fee is what buys the usage right: "Yeah. Correct. I think that's a good distinction."
  • One flat fee for campaign one, $50 or $75. "Let's just do $75 across the board," then "we'll probably go $50 or $75," to be set after hearing Pranav on Sep 10. No tiering built yet.
  • Raw file means the video. "It's just the video." The final version uploaded for review counts.
  • Campaign type is Gifted Product + Fee. "Gifted with fee. Okay, great."
  • New ask: the money goes at the top. "It seems crazy that we're burying that they're paid $100 in the 4th bullet in light gray text." He wants the amount on the offer pill, the invite card, and the brief header, laid out in Figma for him to review.

  1. The contract grants a 90-day license, not ownership. Full usage rights including paid usage, raw files where available, the creator keeps ownership, and any creator fee is passed through at cost, capped at $100 per creator.

    Agreed · signed Aug 10

  2. Tony has called it "full ownership rights." Section 3 of the same contract says content "is owned by the respective creator unless separately agreed." Pranav should hear one version, and it has to be the signed one. Resolved on the Sep 8 call: Tony confirmed the contract's version.

    Resolved · Sep 8

  3. Every creator agrees at accept, and gets paid for it. Tony, Aug 10: "it has to be in the brief, creator agreed to it and get paid, or they don't get the campaign." The per-creator payment on this deal is the rights fee. That makes the creator brief card a consent moment, not a footnote.

    Said · Tony, Aug 10

  4. One field feeds both cards. The brand portal "Usage & Permissions" card and the creator app "Usage and Rights" card both render usage_permissions. Whatever ops types shows on both. So the copy must name the brand and read correctly to a creator and to Pranav at the same time.

    Verified in code · Sep 8

  5. Five things the contract does not say. When the 90 days start, which edits are allowed, ads from the creator's own handle, exclusivity, and renewal. Tony did not touch these on Sep 8. Section 7 proposes a default for each, to confirm on Sep 10.

AgreedPage 1, "What's Included In Your Subscription Today", bullet "Usage Rights"
Brand will receive 90 days of full usage rights, including paid usage rights, for all videos. Videos provided as raw files. Benable will secure as many of those rights free of charge as possible. If a creator requires a usage fee, Benable will bill the brand only for the creator fee actually paid to secure those rights, with no additional markup or charge from Benable. Creator-specific usage fees are not to exceed $100 per creator. Benable will not charge any fees for securing these paid rights.

The paragraph above it: "Any creator usage-rights fees are billed only as pass-through reimbursements for amounts Benable actually pays to creators."

AgreedSection 3, "Content & Intellectual Property"
All content created by creators in connection with Brand Portal campaigns or activities is owned by the respective creator unless separately agreed. Benable retains the right to display, distribute, and promote such content on the Benable platform. For campaign videos, Brand will receive 90-day usage rights, including paid usage rights, with raw files provided where available. Benable will secure as many of those rights free of charge as possible; if a creator requires a usage fee, Benable will bill Brand only for the creator fee actually paid, with no additional markup or charge from Benable.

It grants

  • 90 days of usage
  • Full usage including paid usage, which covers ads run from the brand's own accounts
  • Raw video files, "where available"
  • Benable secures the rights, free where possible
  • Creator fee passed through at cost, never above $100 per creator

It does not grant

  • Ownership. The creator owns the content.
  • Anything perpetual, or "in perpetuity"
  • Use past 90 days without a new agreement
  • A Benable fee for securing rights (there is none)

It is silent on

  • When the 90 days start
  • Which edits are allowed
  • Ads from the creator's own handle (Spark Ads, partnership ads need a creator code)
  • Exclusivity, and resale to third parties
  • Renewal after 90 days, and its price

One field, two cards

Both screenshots from Sep 8 are the same data. The admin textarea "Usage & Permissions" (one line per bullet, on the organization's campaign setup defaults) is stored as brief.usage_permissions. The brand portal renders every line on its card. The creator app renders every line on its card, and only falls back to a built-in sentence when the list is empty.

Consequence. A line that starts "Your brand receives" is read by creators. A line that starts "Brand receives" is read by the brand. Until product adds a creator-facing field, the copy has to name the brand and describe the terms in the third person. That happens to be the honest version too: both sides read the same words, and accept means the same thing on both ends.

Also worth knowing. The generic default was 90 days until April 9, 2026, when it was cut to 30 (commit d4bbe69b3) because creators were declining over it (Sep 2 ad-activation study). Tony's current sales email promises 30 days plus reshare "in perpetuity." So 90 days is right for PCA because PCA bought it, and wrong as the default for everyone else.

The wording

One set of three lines, written for one field that two audiences read. The payment is not on this card. Below it, split versions for when the field splits, and two optional lines.

A. Recommended now: the shared field Terms decided · Sep 8 Wording proposed · Julia

Paste into the organization's Usage & Permissions default, one line per bullet. Zero build. Lines 1 and 2 carry Tony's Sep 8 answers. The amount is deliberately not here: it sits on the payment tile at the top of the brief, where Tony wants it, so this card states the terms and nothing else (Julia, Sep 8). The clock start is Julia's proposal.

usage_permissions · Simple Skincare · 3 lines
  1. Simple Skincare gets full usage rights to each video for 90 days from the day it goes live: organic social, website, emails, and paid ads on their own channels.

B. If the field splits Proposed

When product adds a creator-facing rights field, each side can speak in the second person. Same terms, different pronoun.

Brand portal, brand-facing

3 lines
  1. Your brand receives 90 days of full usage rights to every video from this campaign, including paid ads on your own channels, plus the raw video file.
  2. Every creator agrees to these rights when they accept the campaign. Light edits are fine. Edits that change what a creator says or how they look are not.
  3. Each creator's rights fee is billed at cost on your monthly invoice, with no Benable markup. Need longer than 90 days? Ask us and we will arrange a renewal with the creator.

Creator app, creator-facing

3 lines
  1. Simple Skincare can use your video for 90 days after you post: on their social accounts, website, emails, and in their ads.
  2. You share the original file (the final version you upload for review counts). Your post stays live on your page, and the video stays yours.
  3. They can trim, caption, or add a logo. They can't change what you say or how you look, or pass the video to another brand. Accepting is your agreement to this.

C. Optional lines

Use only if the matching question in section 7 lands that way
  1. Running a video as an ad from the creator's own account (Spark Ads, partnership ads) needs the creator's authorization code. Benable requests it for you. Add only if Pranav confirms they want creator-handle ads.
  2. Each creator's rights fee is billed at cost on your monthly invoice. No Benable markup. Belongs on the fee card in Amine's paid-campaign flow, not on the shared rights card, because creators would read it too.

D. Tony's new ask: the money goes at the top Open · owner Julia, Amine

Tony, Sep 8: "It seems crazy that we're burying that they're paid $100 in the 4th bullet in light gray text. That's a huge value prop." He wants the amount visible before the creator scrolls, on every step, and the whole path laid out in Figma so he can review it: the new-offer pill, the "a brand wants to work with you" card ("about the brand, price, gift plus $75"), and the brief header ("this is like a $75 campaign"). The rights card then states the terms and does not repeat the amount (Julia, Sep 8). This is a scope increase on Amine's paid-campaign screens, which today carry the fee in a tile and this card. Send Tony the Figma before building.

Never on the creator side

  • UGC (decided Aug 7: "content rights")
  • Perpetual, forever, in perpetuity (Tony, Aug 7: "spooks people")
  • Own, ownership (the contract says the creator owns it)
  • Bonus, reward, prize, earned (paid-campaign vocabulary, decided Aug 28: it is "your payment")
  • Whitelisting (industry jargon; say "ads from your account")

Why these lines

  • Every term comes from the contract: 90 days, paid usage, raw files, creator keeps ownership, fee capped at $100.
  • The brand is named, so the same sentence is true on both cards.
  • The payment is not repeated here. It lives on the payment tile at the top of the brief (Tony, Sep 8), and Accept & Commit is already the consent moment, so the card only states the terms.
  • The edits rule is the Aug 7 manual-offer rule, and it must be visible here because creators consent before they film.
  • No dashes anywhere, so the lines can go straight into SMS and email too.
